Haldi & Mehendi Function Planning in Ahmedabad — A Practical Guide for 2026

Nikhil Sharma·1 Oct 2026·10 min read
Home›Blog›Haldi & Mehendi Function Planning in Ahmedabad

By Nikhil Sharma, Founder — Event 1O1

Of all the functions in an Ahmedabad wedding, Haldi and Mehendi tend to get planned last and budgeted least. Yet they are often the ones families remember most — not because of grand production, but because of the energy in the room.

This guide is for families who want both functions done properly: the right sound setup, the right seating, the right decor mood, and a clear sense of what each function actually needs — without overspending on things that will not show on camera or in memory.

At Event 1O1, we have planned and executed wedding functions across Ahmedabad and Gujarat since 2019. What follows is what we have learned works when it comes to Haldi and Mehendi.

Haldi Function — What It Actually Needs

A Haldi is a daytime function. The light is bright, the mood is playful, and the focus is on the family rather than the production. This shapes every decision you make.

Seating for Haldi

Most Haldi functions work best on floor seating — cushions and bolsters (gadde takiye) are comfortable and photogenic. For larger gatherings, having a row of chairs with covers for older family members is a practical addition.

Counter tables for snacks and refreshments are standard. One table per 15–20 guests is a reasonable starting estimate.

Haldi Decor — Setting the Mood

Haldi decor works best when it leans into colour and texture rather than scale. Yellow, orange, and marigold are classics — not because they are trendy, but because they photograph beautifully in natural light.

Typical elements for a well-decorated Haldi:

  • Floral backdrop or entrance arch
  • Marigold string arrangements (fresh, not artificial)
  • Selfie booth or flower gazebo — doubles as the main photo spot
  • Series lights on trees if the venue has outdoor space
  • Carpet or floor covering for the seated area

What you generally do not need at a Haldi: a full truss rig, LED screens, or heavy lighting arrays. Save those for the evening functions.

Haldi Carnival Theme & Kund Seating

For families who want the Haldi to feel more playful and visually distinctive, a carnival-style theme can bring together colourful drapes, floral installations, themed props and a statement seating area. The idea is to create a bright daytime setting that works for the ceremony as well as family photographs without turning the Haldi into an unnecessarily heavy production.

A decorative Haldi kund or kund-style seating setup can become the focal point of the ceremony. It can be styled in a bold contemporary palette such as pink and florals, or in a more traditional look with gold lotus-style seating and flower arrangements. The important part is to leave enough open space around the kund for family participation, photography and movement during the Haldi.

Sound and DJ for Haldi

Sound system requirements for a Haldi depend on venue size, acoustics, ceiling height, whether the space is indoor or outdoor, and how many guests you are covering. A professional sound setup for a Haldi is typically more compact than what an evening Sangeet needs — you want enough coverage for clear music and mic pickup without the volume overwhelming conversation.

Your event team should size the PA system based on your specific venue and guest count. Do not assume a single configuration fits all Haldi functions.

Live dhol is worth considering for the Haldi entry moment — it adds energy that a DJ alone cannot replicate. Many Ahmedabad families add one or two dholis alongside the DJ.

The Haldi Entry

The entry moment — whether the bride is carried on a floral platform, makes a flower-shower arrival, or arrives with a choreographed entry sequence — sets the energy for the whole function.

Special effects such as coloured smoke, cold fog, CO2 jets, or confetti can add visual impact to a Haldi entry. However, any such effects must be confirmed as suitable for your venue, and subject to venue management permission and applicable safety requirements. What works in a large open courtyard may not be appropriate in a closed banquet hall with a low ceiling or limited ventilation. Always discuss SFX with your event team and venue in advance.

Anchor for Haldi

Not every Haldi needs a professional anchor, but one makes a meaningful difference for larger or more structured functions. A good anchor keeps the energy moving between moments, manages the programme, and gives the family a point of contact — so you are not running the function yourselves on the day.

Mehendi Function — Practical Planning

The Mehendi is a transitional function — more relaxed than the Sangeet but more considered than a casual gathering. It sits between the two in terms of production requirements.

Seating for Mehendi

Mehendi functions typically use a combination of floor seating for younger guests and chairs for older attendees. A mix of gadde-takiye and covered chairs works for most family sizes.

The Mehendi artist needs a dedicated setup area with good overhead or focused lighting so the work can be photographed clearly. If you are booking a bridal Mehendi artist separately, confirm their space and lighting requirements in advance.

Mehendi Decor

Mehendi decor can mirror the Haldi palette or shift slightly — sage green, blush, and terracotta work as alternatives to yellow. The key elements are:

  • A dedicated seating area with a backdrop
  • Fresh flowers or potted plants (more durable through a long function)
  • A selfie booth if one was not already used at Haldi
  • Candles or fairy lights for evening Mehendi functions
  • Series lights on trees for outdoor or semi-outdoor venues

Sound and DJ for Mehendi

Mehendi functions typically need a lighter sound footprint than a Sangeet — the music is more ambient than centrestage. However, if your Mehendi runs into the evening or has a programme with performances, the setup should be scaled accordingly.

As with Haldi, the right configuration depends on your venue's acoustic properties, indoor or outdoor setting, and the size of the space. Talk through your requirements with your event team so the sound is sized for your specific function rather than a generic estimate.

When to Combine Mehendi and Sangeet

Some families plan Mehendi and Sangeet on the same day — Mehendi in the afternoon, Sangeet in the evening. This is efficient from a venue and guest travel perspective but requires careful production planning.

If you are combining them, the Mehendi setup is cleared mid-afternoon and the Sangeet production goes in. The changeover window required depends on production scope — how much decor is changing, what equipment needs to be removed and brought in, and how much access the venue gives your production team. This should be discussed clearly with your event team during the planning stage so no function is rushed.

What Changes When the Guest Count Goes Up

The fundamentals — seating, sound, decor, and anchor — remain constant. What scales with guest count is the scope and specification of each element.

  • Smaller functions can be handled with a compact PA, light decoration, and minimal crew.
  • Mid-size functions benefit from a dedicated anchor, fuller decor, and a properly specified sound system covering the entire space.
  • Larger functions may require larger or distributed PA systems, or line-array configurations, depending on venue layout and coverage requirements — your production team should specify this based on the actual space.

If your Haldi has significantly more guests than your Mehendi, treat them as two separate productions with separate scopes — because they are.

What to Confirm Before You Book Anything

This checklist applies regardless of which vendor you work with:

Venue:

  • Is the hall air-conditioned? (Affects heat load from equipment and lighting)
  • What is the power supply capacity and load limit?
  • Are there restrictions on DJ volume, special effects, or open flames?
  • Is the floor even enough for a dance floor if required?

Event dates and timing:

  • What time does the Haldi start and end?
  • Is the Mehendi the same day or the following day?
  • If same day — how much time is available between functions for changeover?

Guest count:

  • Separate confirmed counts for Haldi and Mehendi
  • Any specific seating requirements for older guests or VIPs?

Sound:

  • Indoor or outdoor venue? What are the acoustic conditions?
  • Are there any sound-level restrictions from the venue or local authority?

Decor:

  • Fresh flowers or artificial? (Fresh photographs better but requires same-day delivery coordination)
  • Colour theme confirmed?
  • Are you bringing your own decor vendor or looking for one?

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the cost of a Haldi or Mehendi function determined?

Haldi and Mehendi function costs depend on a number of factors: guest count, venue type and size, the scope of sound and DJ services, decor requirements, whether you need an anchor, and any special entry arrangements. Rather than working from a fixed package price, we recommend getting a scope-based quote that reflects your actual function requirements. Contact Event 1O1 to discuss your specific plans and we will give you an honest, itemised estimate.

Can Mehendi and Sangeet be planned on the same day?

Yes, and many Ahmedabad families do this well. The Mehendi typically runs in the afternoon and the Sangeet in the evening. The key requirement is a clear changeover window between the two functions — how long that needs to be depends on how much decor and equipment is changing, and how much access the venue provides to your production team. This should be planned in advance with your event team, not decided on the day.

Do we need an anchor for the Haldi?

Not always. For smaller, more informal Haldi functions, one family member managing the flow is often enough. For larger functions — or where you have a structured entry sequence, performances, or multiple family moments — a professional anchor helps keep the programme on time and takes pressure off the family on the day.

What should we look for when booking a Mehendi artist?

For bridal Mehendi, look for an artist whose portfolio shows detailed work consistent with the style you want — Rajasthani, Arabic, or contemporary fusion each have different characteristics. Confirm their setup requirements: space, a stable working surface, and good lighting are non-negotiable. Book well in advance during wedding season (October to February in Gujarat), and confirm whether family Mehendi for other guests is included or separately priced.

Are special effects like smoke or CO2 appropriate for Haldi?

Special effects — including coloured smoke, cold fog, CO2 jets, or confetti — can add visual energy to a Haldi entry or moment. Whether they are appropriate depends entirely on your venue: the size of the space, ceiling height, ventilation, and the venue's own permissions and safety requirements. Some effects that work well in open courtyards or large banquet halls are not suitable in smaller or enclosed spaces. Always discuss any SFX requirements with your event team and get explicit confirmation from your venue before planning them into your programme.

What is the difference between a Haldi and a Mehendi in terms of production scope?

A Haldi is typically a daytime function with a more casual, playful energy — the production is lighter, decor leans on colour and flowers rather than heavy structure, and sound requirements are moderate. A Mehendi is usually more seated and ambient, with the focus on the Mehendi application and family time. The Sangeet, by contrast, is a full evening production with stage, lighting, and a complete sound rig. Understanding this difference helps families budget appropriately and avoid either underpreparing for a Haldi or overspending on one.
